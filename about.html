What I believe

AI adoption is a curiosity problem

The teams that make AI stick aren't the ones with the cleanest code. They're the ones willing to pilot a tool for two days inside a real project, find where it breaks, and iterate. Curiosity is the input variable. Everything else is downstream.

And an architecture problem

Training a team on tools they don't have infrastructure for is teaching people to drive without giving them a road. Once files, projects, and information are structured so AI can use them, the team teaches themselves. Without that, no workshop sticks.

Build first, then govern

You don't need a Chief AI Officer signing model risk policies for infrastructure that doesn't exist yet. You need a builder who slows down to speed up: onboarding, logins, piloting alongside daily work. The unglamorous foundation is the work.

There's no "right way" to do creative ops

There's only what fits the people in the room. A great engagement is an art of pulling different levers: playing to strengths, noticing where someone's struggling versus learning, finding the tech that fits the grooves of an existing business.
